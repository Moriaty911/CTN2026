02

Choose the reply purpose and prompt type

Choose one primary task for the selected target. Tone, topic and account voice are separate parameters.

Reply candidate from Layer 1

What should this reply accomplish?

Identify one question or claim to address, then select a suitable prompt type. The briefs below are proposed template tasks.

↓
Each card includes its evidence requirement and fallback.
P01 · SUPPORT

Support & supplement

When: The post aligns with our position and there is a useful addition.

Requires: A clear basis for support and a specific additional point.

Prompt brief & fallback

Briefly support the relevant point and add one useful explanation drawn from the supplied material.

Fallback: If nothing useful can be added, reassess a like or no interaction.

P02 · ANSWER

Answer a direct question

When: A concrete question can be answered from available material.

Requires: The exact question and sufficient supporting information.

Prompt brief & fallback

Answer the question first, then give the necessary basis. Preserve qualifications in the source.

Fallback: Ask a specific clarification if possible; otherwise request material or defer.

P03 · ARGUMENT

Respond to an argument

When: There is a clear argument and a supported response.

Requires: The original claim and relevant reasons or evidence.

Prompt brief & fallback

Represent the claim accurately and respond to one central reason using the supplied material.

Fallback: Clarify an unclear claim or request evidence; do not invent a counterexample.

P04 · PROPOSALS

Compare policy proposals

When: Opposing authors suggest different approaches to the same policy issue.

Requires: Both proposals and enough context to compare their scope.

Prompt brief & fallback

Present the specific proposals and ask about their conditions, exceptions or reconciliation. Keep the conclusion tied to those authors.

Fallback: Clarify scope when missing. Proposal differences alone do not prove factual error.

P05 · CROSS-CHECK

Cross-check conflicting posts

When: Comparable factual statements conflict, but their accuracy is unresolved.

Requires: Both original posts, matching subject and relevant time or conditions.

Prompt brief & fallback

Accurately contrast the two statements and ask which version or evidence supports them. Preserve uncertainty about which is correct.

Fallback: If the statements cannot be compared, clarify the missing condition or drop this route.

P06 · CORRECT

Correct with verified evidence

When: Verified material supports a correction to a specific factual claim.

Requires: A verified source applicable to the same subject, time and scope.

Prompt brief & fallback

State the specific correction and its source. Keep the conclusion within what the source establishes.

Fallback: Use cross-checking or clarification, or defer. Model confidence alone cannot establish truth.

P07 · CONCERN

Address an expressed concern

When: Strong emotion accompanies a concrete concern and a response opportunity.

Requires: An identifiable concern and material for the substantive response.

Prompt brief & fallback

Acknowledge the concern actually expressed, then address its specific issue in a calm, grounded way.

Fallback: Reassess no interaction for repeated abuse or a thread with no useful response opportunity.

P08 · CLARIFY

Ask a focused clarification

When: The claim, scope or basis is unclear, but a concrete question can be formulated.

Requires: A clear question target and an identifiable information gap.

Prompt brief & fallback

Ask one focused question about the missing scope, condition or basis. Avoid assuming the answer.

Fallback: If the target itself is unclear because essential context is absent, request it upstream or defer.

P09 · SUMMARIZE

Organize the discussion

When: Several relevant comments can be usefully organized.

Requires: Traceable comments and a known sample scope.

Prompt brief & fallback

Separate agreement, disagreement and unresolved questions in the supplied comments. State the sample scope where relevant.

Fallback: Do not claim a thread-wide consensus from a limited sample.

Select one primary type. Account voice, topic, emotional tone and response length can be combined with the same template.

03

Interpret disagreement before choosing a response

Compare explicit post pairs. Preserve current and historical post IDs, authors, original text, conditions, assessment versions and search scope.

RelationshipMeaningPossible response route
Agreement / elaborationThe claims align or one adds compatible detail.Continue ordinary response evaluation.
Different value prioritiesThe authors care about different considerations; both may be compatible.Respond to a concrete concern or argument. This difference does not establish inaccuracy.
Different policy proposalsThe authors recommend different actions for a comparable issue.P04 · Compare proposals and ask about conditions or reconciliation.
Incompatible factual statementsBoth statements cannot be correct under the same subject, time and conditions.P05 · Cross-check while accuracy is unresolved. P06 · Correct only with applicable verified evidence.
Different conditions / unclear contextVersion, time, scope, quotation or meaning prevents a reliable comparison.P08 · Clarify when a concrete question is possible; otherwise request context.
No history / no match foundNo supplied historical material means unknown; no detected conflict is limited to the searched sample.Continue other evaluations and retain the comparison limits.
Hypothetical example · proposal disagreement

Different recommendations

A: “Remove proof requirements from every public venue.”
B: “Remove them from parks, but keep them in hospitals.”

Route: P04 · Ask how the proposed exceptions should work. The disagreement concerns what should be done.

Hypothetical example · factual conflict

Different descriptions of one policy

A: “This policy version includes parks.”
B: “The same policy version excludes parks.”

Route: P05 · Ask for the policy text. Use P06 only if verified material supports a specific correction.

Prioritize cross-author comparisons for this design. Track changes by the same author separately. Different authors do not automatically represent one organization or a unified official position. Keep platform observations separate from verified reference material.

04

Deliver a grounded generation task

The LLM receives the selected task and its materials, alongside the assessment. Prompt-type labels and scores alone are insufficient.

Proposed handoff contents

  • Action & target Candidate action, target post or comment ID, original text and context.
  • Purpose & prompt type One primary task and the exact question or claim to address.
  • Evidence Paired historical posts, source references and verification status.
  • Limits Missing context, uncertainty and the scope of the comparison.
  • Expression Account requirements, downstream-confirmed voice, tone and length.
  • Versions Decision, question-set and prompt-template versions.
