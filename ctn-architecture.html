01Design Premise: Two Scarce Resources, Not One

Almost every bot architecture assumes that compute is the bottleneck and focuses on saving tokens. That assumption is wrong for this competition.

ResourceScarcityBasisCan Money Buy More?
Platform Read BudgetVery scarceEstimated from public 2025 data: per team ≈ 0.61 req/s (reads + writes combined)❌ Not purchasable
Platform Write BudgetScarcestIn 2025, only about 11.4% of requests were posts (7.07M posts / 62M requests)❌ Not purchasable
Jev InferenceAbundant$0.042 / 1M tokens; free output; external API calls do not consume the platform rate limit✅ Buy freely
LLM GenerationAbundantExternal models explicitly permitted (Rules 5.1 / 5.2)✅ Purchasable
The Entire Point of the Design Convert scarce platform reads into high-value platform writes.
Jev is the only abundant resource between the two. It is not a money-saving filter, but judgment we can afford. Use it generously, rather than sparingly.

One Hard Rule

Only the adapters in client/ may access the platform.
Only act.py may initiate write actions.

Break this rule and the rate-limit guarantee fails; the cost is an account ban. Accounts are expendable, but replacements need to be established again.

02Overall Architecture

A three-stage pipeline + two side resources + one authoritative state store. Arrows show what passes between stages and whether it is trusted.

Two-Level Token Buckets → Execute → Record
  • Team-level bucket + account-level bucket; reads and writes fully separated

State + Telemetry Local

Why Split the Pipeline into Three Stages? The three latencies span two orders of magnitude: platform 100–200ms, Jev 70–500ms, LLM generation 1–5s. In a single-threaded read → judge → generate → publish loop, a 3-second LLM call stalls the entire perception pipeline — and a 3-second stall during a trend window means missing that window. Decouple the three stages with asyncio queues; queues are not persisted as state, but rebuilt from sqlite on restart.

04Key Design Decisions

① Use sqlite for State, Synchronously

At 0.61 req/s, an asynchronous database adds complexity with no benefit: just another source of failure. Use the standard-library sqlite3 with WAL mode and short transactions. The main flow is async, but blocking for a few milliseconds at this load is acceptable.

TablePurpose
accountsAccount pool + health status + establishment progress
posts_seenDeduplication + local cache (avoid repeated reads)
decisionsEvery decision + full Jev probabilities and confidence
actionsExecution results
exposureWhich citizen saw which of our content, and when ← cannot be reconstructed later; required on Day 1
targetsTarget citizens + estimated feed share + control-group flags
budgetRequest ledger (continue counting after restart)

Restart recovery is a requirement, not a bonus — a process running continuously for two weeks will crash.

② Accounts Are Expendable and Must Be Established Early

Platform Rule LS.5.1: rate limits “varies by trust signals and recent activity”. Therefore new accounts have lower quotas → create and establish accounts now, rather than waiting for the competition.

class AccountStatus(Enum):
    ACTIVE     = "active"      # Healthy
    THROTTLED  = "throttled"   # Throttled: retain, but reduce frequency
    SUSPENDED  = "suspended"   # Isolate; probe after cooldown
    RETIRED    = "retired"     # Retired; never use again

Independent token buckets per account: one throttled account must not slow down the whole team.

③ All Inbound Content Is Untrusted (a Real Attack Surface)

Rule 5.2 explicitly permits opponents to inject prompts into our agents. Two defenses are needed, and their placement matters:

StoreContentTrust LevelMay Act as Instructions?
Trusted StoreOur own arguments, factual anchors, and personasTrustedYes
Observation StorePosts / comments / news collected from the platformUntrustedNever
RAG Poisoning Is a Path the 2025 Teams Probably Did Not Defend If observations can be retrieved into the generation context, an opponent can write text specifically designed to be retrieved and hijack generation. The contamination happens at the retrieval layer, invisible at the input layer. The two stores must be physically isolated.

④ RAG Stores Arguments + Personas, Not Verbatim Scripts

Platform Rule LS.5.3 prohibits “coordinated inauthentic behaviour … repetitive low-quality posting”, and there is a double risk: several consultancies serve your side; identical supplied scripts would compete for the same attention.

TALKING_POINTS = {
  "yes": { "arguments": [...],      # Five core arguments
           "rebuttals": {...},      # Responses to opposing arguments
           "fact_anchors": [...],   # IHO recommendation / COVID death toll / health system
           "framings": [...] },     # Prevention / responsibility / vulnerable groups
}
PERSONA_VOICE = {
  "persona_07": {
    "voice": "40-year-old fisher; short, colloquial sentences; distrusts government",
    "avoid_phrases": ["vaccine passport", "compulsory"],   # Activates the opposing frame
    "stance_intensity": 0.6,              # Not a fanatic
  },
}
# Generation: one argument × 100 personas = 100 surface realizations

Add a diversity guard: if a draft exceeds the similarity threshold against our recent content, regenerate with stronger persona constraints. This single mechanism addresses platform coordination detection, collisions with allied teams, and a bot network that visibly looks like a bot network.

⑤ Narrowcasting: Story Score Credit Is Shared

The organizers' paper states: “the system attributed credit to all teams whose posts the NPC had consumed since their previous state update”.

If a citizen reads 10 teams' content between state updates, those 10 teams equally share credit for the opinion change. Therefore:

Appear once each before 1,000 citizens  ≪  Appear 10 times each before 100 citizens

This directly affects the perception layer: monitoring global trends alone gives us the same public information as everyone else, without differentiation. Spend read budget to measure our share of particular citizens' feeds.

# Maintain a coverage estimate for each target citizen
target.feed_share = my_posts_seen / total_posts_seen
priority = target.persuadable * (1 - target.feed_share) * target.reach

05The Two-Stage Jev Funnel

Jev's key feature: one request can contain multiple questions, evaluated in parallel internally; extra questions add almost no latency, only a little input-token cost. Amortize the overhead.

Stage A · Batch Triage

Process a batch of posts in one request. 24 questions = keep-or-discard judgments for 24 posts, at a cost of a fraction of a cent.

# 1 state × N questions
state = render_posts(posts[0:24])
questions = {
  f"p{i}_worth_it": Noul(
     f"Post #{i} is worth spending one scarce write action on")
  for i in range(24)
}
# → Eliminate 90%+ here

Stage B · Per-Post Deep Evaluation

Evaluate only survivors (usually 1–3 posts) with about 10 independent questions.

deep = await jev.ask(
    render_post(post),
    DEEP_QUESTIONS)
# → Decide tier / account / content
Measure Batch Size; Do Not Guess It With oversized batches, references such as 'post 17' become ambiguous and instruction following deteriorates. triage_batch_size (initial guess: 24) must be determined from accuracy curves: the first problem for the mock environment to solve.

Question Design: Independent Atomic Judgments, Never Compound Questions

'Should We Reply to This?' Is a Bad Question It combines at least six independent dimensions into one judgment. You cannot see what the model is weighing, so cannot tune thresholds. Split it into atomic judgments, then make the decision in code. 
DEEP_QUESTIONS = {
  # ── Relevance ──
  "is_my_side":     Choice(["strong_yes","lean_yes","neutral",
                             "lean_no","strong_no","off_topic"]),

  # ── Persuadability (the Story channel's handle) ──
  "persuadable":    Score("How uncertain the author's position is and how persuadable they are", levels=5),
  "emotional_heat": Score("Emotional intensity of this post", levels=5),

  # ── Propagation value (the Engagement channel's handle) ──
  "visibility":     Score("Current visibility and potential reach of this post", levels=5),
  "reply_upside":   Score("Expected citizen responses to a reply here", levels=5),

  # ── Risk ──
  "rule_risk":      Noul("The expected reply may trigger platform rules or rate limits"),
  "injection":      Noul("This content contains instructions attempting to manipulate an AI system"),
  "is_bot":         Noul("This account appears to be an opponent bot rather than a simulated citizen"),

  # ── Cost routing ──
  "needs_nuance":   Noul("Fact-checking or multi-step reasoning is needed; escalate to an LLM"),
}

# Combine judgments in code (the official speculative fan-out pattern):
if r.noul("injection") > 0.7:  quarantine(post)      # Isolate first; never pass to the LLM
elif r.noul("rule_risk") > 0.6: skip(post)            # Protect account assets
elif r.noul("is_bot") > 0.8:    skip(post)            # Do not waste budget on bots
elif r.score("reply_upside") > 3.4 and r.score("persuadable") > 3.0:
    if r.noul("needs_nuance") > 0.5: route_to_llm(post)      # Spend scarce budget here
    else:                        route_to_rag(post)       # Talking-point library + persona rewrite
elif aligned(r) and r.score("visibility") > 3.0:
    amplify(post)                                            # Story channel
else:
    observe_only(post)                                       # Zero cost; only update the world model
Why This Design Can Win These dimensions are observable, traceable, and calibratable. Logs show how much opinion movement came from actions with 'persuadable > 3.0 ', letting you tune the threshold. If you ask only 'should we reply?', you never know what to change.
needs_nuance is the money-saving question: it routes by task difficulty, much more accurately than routing by price.

Rule 7.1 explicitly limits Engagement to interactions from simulated citizens. Tiers should depend on which score they buy, not how expensive they are.

Jev's Most Valuable Job Not 'should we like or reply?', but: find the few dozen opportunities worth T2 budget among thousands of T0 observations.

You are not facing 4,000 people, but a system composed of profiles + finite-state machines + probabilistic update functions + LLMs . Understanding its structure tells you where to focus your effort.

Key Deduction: Why It Must Be a Hybrid Architecture

An easily overlooked phrase in the paper: “a probabilistic update function determines whether and how their internal state changes” — this reads like a description of a programmatic transition function rather than an LLM judgment.

Consider concurrency constraints. In 2025, 12 concurrent LLM instances served 4,000 NPCs. A rough calculation:

At ~2s per generation, throughput ≈ 12 / 2 =  6  calls/second
4 weeks = 2,419,200 seconds → about  14.5 million LLM calls
Per NPC: about 3,600 calls / 4 weeks ≈  130  calls/day

This budget is not generous, but it does not imply that every state transition requires an LLM call. My judgment: Layer 4 is probably a programmatic probabilistic function; the LLM mainly handles Layer 5 generation and some relevance judgments between Layers 2 and 3.

What This Means for Strategy If Layer 4 Is Programmatic (my preferred hypothesis) → the deciding factor is not eloquence, but content's computable features: position distance, repetition count, source consistency, visible engagement, and recency.
If Layer 4 Uses LLM Judgments → emphasis shifts to framing, tone, identity resonance, and argument quality.

🎯 Recommendation: prioritize strategies effective under both hypotheses. Most actions under the five gates below work either way. The main experimental distinction is which framing and wording to use, something to measure on D1–D2.

Matching Interests at Each Gate

Gate ① · Enter the Feed: The Graph Matters More Than Eloquence

NPCs see their own feeds. Your content must come from someone they follow, be reposted by someone they follow, or appear in trends.

  • Follow First, Speak Later. The 40 bots should establish connections with target citizens before publishing: get in position first, then act.
  • Go Through Accounts They Trust. Let citizens' own posts carry your arguments, and amplify them. Arguments conveyed by people like them have very different credibility.
  • Trends Are the Public Lane; the Follow Graph Is the Private Lane. The private lane has far fewer opponents, and no allied teams competing with you.
2026 Closes the Fabricated Social Proof Route Engagement must come from simulated citizens. You can no longer use your own accounts' mutual likes to fake widespread agreement — the low-quality 2025 tactic. What remains is genuine citizen engagement and follow relationships. This makes establishing the follow graph an unavoidable first step.

Gate ② · Pass the Attention Filter: Make the Point Clear

The NPC's FSM scans the feed in passive-browsing mode. What turns a glance into actual reading and a reaction?

  • Short, Specific, with an Emotional Hook. The first line decides whether it survives.
  • Relevant to Their Existing Lives: work, family, commuting, healthcare, children's schools—not abstract policy arguments. The 40-dimensional profile includes employment, family, and economic status: ready-made handles.
  • Avoid Abstract Vocabulary. 'Compulsory vaccination certification' is policy language; 'How do I get to work tomorrow?' is everyday language. The latter gets read.

Gate ③ · Pass the Acceptance Filter: The Easiest Place to Go Wrong 📙

Here is a possibly counterintuitive suggestion. Social judgment theory includes the concept of latitude of acceptance: people accept only a limited range of positions.An argument outside their latitude triggers a contrast effect: instead of accepting it, they move in the opposite direction. In other words, an overly forceful rebuttal can push moderates toward the other side.

Audience❌ Wrong Approach✅ Right Approach
Committed OpponentsDirectly argue for your side's benefitsMove on. Or discuss only common ground (such as 'vaccination is voluntary')
Moderates Leaning Against YouSay 'you are wrong'Start from values they already share (tourism, personal freedom, concern about government overreach); move only a small step
Completely NeutralPush your position hardOffer a frame in which both sides' concerns are heard, lowering the psychological cost of changing position
Leaning Toward Your SideRepeat the argumentReinforce + provide responses to opposing arguments, enabling them to defend their position
📙 Core Strategy Do not try to persuade everyone; help those already leaning toward you feel more confident and willing to speak.
  • They face the lowest cost of changing position: work with the current, not against it
  • They become your propagation nodes: statements from citizens are an order of magnitude more credible than statements from your bots
  • This feeds both Engagement (citizen interactions) and Story (reinforced opinions): both scores rise together

Gate ④ · Trigger Updates: Repetition and Distribution, Not One Viral Post

  • Repeat, but Vary the Surface Form. The same argument reaches one NPC through 20 accounts, 20 formulations, at different times. One exposure to one perspective is usually insufficient.
  • Consistent Sources > One High-Quality Post. Hearing the same point from three directions is much more credible than hearing one person repeat it three times.
  • ⚠️ Watch for Backfire. Mechanically repeating similar wording in a short period can trigger coordinated inauthentic behavior detection or NPC resistance: precisely why the diversity guard exists.

Gate ⑤ · Make It Last: Increase Effort Late

The paper explicitly states that opinion changes carry more weight closer to voting day; the NPC's FSM is also periodic, updating state at discrete times.

  • Stay Present Rather Than Relying on One Burst. Appear repeatedly at the moments when the NPC is browsing.
  • Increase Effort Late. But do not skip the early follow graph and reputation: otherwise they will not see what you say later. Build the audience early; concentrate influence late.

Implementation: Clustering Instead of Individual Customization

Customizing for each of 4,000 citizens is impractical. Matching their interests becomes:

4000 NPC  ──► Cluster using 40-dimensional profiles ──►  8–15 archetypes
          ──► One argument frame + matching bot persona per archetype
         ──► Produce content by archetype; deliver through accounts resembling them

Not 4,000 custom texts, but 15 carefully matched frame × persona combinations delivered to the corresponding citizen groups. This addresses persuasion efficiency and coordination detection at once.

08Measurement Design ← Finalize Before Writing Code

Define measurement criteria first so the code captures the right fields. Reversing the order misses critical data.

First Clarify: Three Different Ways of 'Winning Them Over' Can Diverge

(a)Engagement: likes, reposts, commentsEngagementEasy, but cheap and gameable

Most 2025 Teams Got Stuck on (a) The paper states: “Because engagement was rewarded, most teams prioritised high-volume posting over nuanced influence.”
Another 7 teams admitted that they deliberately abandoned their original plans for coherent, nuanced influence and switched to flooding the platform because its reward structure favored quantity over quality.

The 2026 rules restrict Engagement to simulated citizens— almost certainly the organizers closing this loophole. Do not repeat the same mistake.

The Attribution Problem and Its Solution

The organizers' scoring already shares credit (see §04-⑤), so even they cannot attribute it to one post. The solution is to create a clean comparison ourselves:

Reserve 10–15% of target citizens as a control group, leaving them untouched throughout. Invest intensively in the remaining treatment group, then compare opinion movement between groups.

  • Sacrifice a little coverage for a causal estimate: treatment-group movement − control-group movement = your true effect
  • Audience segmentation: which citizens actually respond
  • Argument selection: compare effects after removing noise from opponents and allied teams
  • Control and treatment groups must be stratified and matched by initial position and persuadability, or the comparison is meaningless

Three Things to Prepare Before the Competition

🔴 ① Exposure Logs: The One Thing You Cannot Reconstruct Later

Record which citizen saw which of our content, and when. The paper says scoring considers all posts consumed since the citizen's previous state update. Replicating that attribution requires our own consumption records.Unrecorded data is lost permanently.

CREATE TABLE exposure (
  ts          REAL,      -- Timestamp
  citizen     TEXT,      -- Which citizen
  my_post_id  TEXT,      -- Which of our content
  my_account  TEXT,
  channel     TEXT,      -- feed / reply / repost / search
  decision_id TEXT
);

This also means prioritizing citizen-feed reads (get_feed(citizen)), not just global trends: we need to know what they saw.

🟠 ② Preregistered Predictions: The Mechanism for Falsifying Our Own Assumptions

Write judgments down before the competition and let the data challenge them. Otherwise, every result becomes 'I knew it' afterward (post-hoc rationalization + multiple comparisons).

# PREDICTIONS.md: zero cost before the competition; highest-value preparation
"Among citizens aged 35–55 with stable employment, the hospital-capacity frame shifts positions more than the personal-freedom frame (predicted difference > 0.05 position units)"

"Highly emotional posts receive more engagement but less opinion movement (i.e., (a) and (b) are negatively correlated)"

"Citing IHO authority works for those already leaning YES, but is ineffective or backfires among NO supporters"

If prediction 2 holds, it directly shows that engagement ≠ winning the referendum, justifying a shift from volume to persuasion. This also forms part of the presentation: 'Data falsified three pre-competition assumptions, so we adjusted strategy on Day 3.'

🟢 ③ In-Distribution Simulation: Useful, with Limits

The paper provides NPC specifications: 40-dimensional profiles (age, gender, family and employment, economic circumstances, baseline positions on controversial and non-controversial issues, initial political inclination). Behavior is driven by a finite-state machine, with internal state changes after interaction determined by a probabilistic update function.

✅ What You Know❌ What You Do Not Know
The dimensions of NPC profilesThe organizers' NPC prompts
NPCs are LLM-drivenWhich models they use
Behavior uses a state machine + probabilistic updatesThe exact form of the update function
Both sides' core arguments (from the setting page)The distribution of positions among 4,000 citizens

The fundamental problem: NPC reactions are f(profile, organizer prompt, organizer model), and you can replicate only the first input. This tests your own citizen model, not theirs. There will be systematic bias, and you will not know its direction.

The Right Use of Simulation Not validating effectiveness, but finding obvious mistakes: ambiguity, a message read as supporting the opposite position, implausible persona voice, or platform-rule violations.
Reliable for filtering obviously bad content; unreliable for deciding which argument is stronger.

After Launch: The First 24–48 Hours Are the Real Measurement Window

PhaseGoalActions
D1–D2Establish Baseline and ControlsLots of OBSERVE, a little PUBLISH; measure citizen distributions and test preregistered predictions
D3–D5Screen ArgumentsUse small controlled experiments to rank argument effects and drop ineffective ones
D6–D11Concentrate on What WorksScale what has been validated
D12–D14Final PushPaper: greater weight closer to voting day → concentrate influence here
Method and Scoring Naturally Align Because late changes carry more weight, early exploration is cheap and later exploitation is valuable. Learning first and exploiting later is not just sound methodology, but optimal for scoring.
Two Cautions 1. Do not burn account quotas on exploration. Its cost should mainly be reads and a little content exposure; do not spend the credibility of 40 accounts just to measure effects.
2. Do not trust weekly polls: in 2025 they randomly sampled 357 NPCs (95% CI). A sample of 357 out of 4,000 has coarse resolution and misses small effects. Instead, track individual target citizens' positions directly (infer from their posts), another use of Jev Score .

Over two weeks, every external dependency will fail. This table matters more than a feature list.

Jev API UnavailableFall back to a conservative rules engine; take only obvious, high-confidence actions; do not take risks
Platform Returns 429Token buckets automatically slow down; cut reads first and protect writes
LLM TimeoutFall back to RAG templates + persona rewriting
Account BannedRemove from pool and reallocate without stopping the pipeline
Process Crashsystemd restarts the process; restore from sqlite
Budget ExhaustedRaise perception thresholds automatically; read only content very likely to produce an action
Core Principle Better to Do Less Than Act Recklessly. Recklessness causes irreversible costs (bans and rule violations); doing less only means fewer points.
